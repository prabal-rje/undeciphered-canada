30 March 1885 · Sir John A. Macdonald Era (1873-1896)

Crozier's cipher telegram from Prince Albert after the Battle of Duck Lake and burning of Fort Carlton

Superintendent L. N. F. (S. G.) Crozier, NWMP (Prince Albert) to NWMP Commissioner A. G. Irvine via William Mackay, HBC Battleford · 30 March 1885

War & Spies
Open interactive decoder → View historical publication on Internet Archive ↗

Historical context

Dramatic front-line NWMP cipher dispatch written four days after the opening battle of the 1885 North-West Resistance at Duck Lake and the accidental burning of Fort Carlton.

Decoded message (plain text)

Riel warned all settlers from farms or they would be forced to join him or be shot, giving 48 hours' notice. People flocking in. Irvine appointed me supply officer. Send 2,000 sacks flour via Calgary to Edmonton and 4,000 lbs bacon if safe. Carlton burned to the ground; saved bulk of provisions. Population all in arms; just got our dead in from Duck Lake, nine in all.

Original secret code

Header: 'Prince Albert, 30th March, 1885. Dear Sir, Telegraph the following cipher in my name to the Commissioner.'
