9 May 1919 · Laurier & World War I (1897-1919)

Ottawa corrects telegraph confusion over 18,000-word German boundary addendum to Versailles

George W. Yates, Prime Minister's Office (Ottawa) to Prime Minister Sir Robert L. Borden (Hotel Astoria, Paris), cable PB24 · 9 May 1919

Government & Politics
Open interactive decoder → View original microfilm at Library and Archives Canada / Héritage Canadiana ↗

Historical context

Captures the logistical confusion on transatlantic circuits as the massive Treaty of Versailles text was transmitted in fragments between Paris, Washington, and Ottawa.

Decoded message (plain text)

Summary of paired draft (folios 63697-63698): Telegraph staff clarify that the 17,000-18,000 words arriving since Wednesday were not the full Treaty of Versailles, but an addendum defining German boundaries for mapmaking, and Washington confirms the full text has not yet reached Ottawa.

Original secret code

PB24. Traveller pitiful orphaned state misinformed as to glover troublesome unlawfully having commenced to arrive Wednesday stop Knower upbraid patent to be an addition to section restrainer uncommon bustlings pain Cracow to enable maps to be made...
