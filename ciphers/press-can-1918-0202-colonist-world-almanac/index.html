Historical context

Published verbatim on the Pacific coast of Canada in Victoria's Daily British Colonist on 2 February 1918 as wartime intelligence proof of a 29 April 1915 Nauen-to-Sayville German Foreign Office wireless order preceding the sinking of RMS Lusitania. Scholarly caveat: while the newspaper faithfully reproduced John R. Rathom's February 1918 World's Work disclosure and its exact page-line-word book-cipher format, modern intelligence historians regard several of Rathom's sensational Providence Journal wireless intercepts as embellished or unverified wartime propaganda reconstructions rather than independently archived German naval intercepts.

Decoded message (plain text)

Warn Lusitania passengers through press not voyage across Atlantic.

Original secret code

From Berlin Foreign Office. To Botschaft, Washington. 9 (44-W): Welt nineteen-fifteen warne 175 29 1 stop 175 1 2 stop durch 622 2 4 stop 19 7 18 stop LIX 11 3, 4, 5, 6.
