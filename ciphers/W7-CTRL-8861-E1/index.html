1866-04-20 · Border, Civil War & Confederation (1816-1872)

Meade reports Fenian lighthouse burning on British side, interview with Fenian Maj. Sinnott on New Brunswick revolution/fisheries, and meeting British Gen. Doyle, Admiral, and Gov. of New Brunswick

Maj. Gen. George G. Meade, Eastport, Maine to Lt. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant, Washington, D.C. · 1866-04-20

Historical context

Decoded message (plain text)

4:30 P.M. Eastport, Me., April 20, 1866, 4 P.M. Lt. Gen. Grant. Have here 4 companies of artillery, one at Calais, three at Eastport. Everything quiet except the burning of a light last night on the British side charged to Fenians. Seized arms removed to Fort Preble. Killian left yesterday for Washington. Had an interview this morning with Major Sinnott, a Fenian, who declared their object to be to foment a revolution in New Brunswick and to settle the fishery question. Gave him distinctly to understand I should not permit any arming or organizing. Yesterday at Calais received visit from Maj. Genl. Doyle, who is here with the Admiral of the Fleet and the Governor of New Brunswick. Much excitement and irritation on both sides of the line. Geo. G. Meade, M.G.

Original secret code

430 PM Eastport Me Apl 20 / 66 Hd Qrs Mil Division Atlantic Eastport Me 4 PM 20th Lt Gen Grant reported yesterday by mail ---- have here four <insertion>4</insertion> companies of artillery one at Calias other three <insertion>3</insertion> at this place ---- Having no means transportation and there being several places in this vicinity requiring watching which can be reached by water I have retained the small steamer that brought myself and one company from Portland ; Everything quiet since my last report except the burning of a Light last night on the British side charged to Fenians ; The arms seized were yesterday removed Fort Preble, boxes contain carbines & muskets with the necessary equipment and ammunition, owner an Irishmen from New York disclaims any connection with fenians or any intention to violate neutrality laws but cannot explain grounds on which he a stranger expected find market in Eastport for such articles nor the coincidence of his arrival with his goods simultaneously with the concentration here of the Fenians ; Killian the acknowledged leader left yesterday in the Portland boat going tis understood to Washn where I trust he will be clearly informed of the views and intentions of the Gov't ---- Had an interview this morning with Major Sinnott a fenian who declared object of the concentration here to be purely political & peaceful their object being to forment a resolution in New Brunswick & to settle the fishery question ---- Gave him distinctly understand I should not permit any arming or organizing for these or any other purposes ---- I yesterday whilst at Calias recd visit from Maj Genl Doyle who is here with the Admiral of the Fleet and the Governor of New Brunswick ---- Much excitement & irritation on both sides of the line but I think my arrival with the troops & the decided attitude I have taken will in a short time quiet matters sig Geo G Meade MG 2.50 P. M. Eastport Me Apr. 25th 1866 No 3 Eastport 1 P. M. 25th Gen Hancock Balto . T
