29 October 1900 · Laurier & World War I (1897-1919)

Fredericton Junction, nine days before voting: Blair turns on Tarte

Railways Minister A. G. Blair to PM Wilfrid Laurier · 29 October 1900

Unread until nowGovernment & Politics

Historical context

Wired from a railway junction in New Brunswick nine days before the November 1900 election, A. G. Blair complained sharply to Laurier that Quebec lieutenant J. Israël Tarte had called Blair's policy purely personal and unsupported by government. The breach never closed: Tarte was dismissed in 1902 and Blair resigned in 1903.

Decoded message (plain text)

“Announcement by Minister [of] Public Works [J. Israël Tarte] that my action [is] purely personal and not supported by Government is doing me more serious harm than any thing which has occurred. It is a straight contradiction of what I have been publicly stating [and] of what I notified railway just [a] year ago.”
