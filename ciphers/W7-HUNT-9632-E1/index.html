1863-12-26 · Border, Civil War & Confederation (1816-1872)

Winthrop E. Hilton's counterfeit Confederate bank-note plates and dies to be shipped via Halifax to Nassau

War Department (Washington, D.C.) to Benjamin Franklin, Chief of Police of Philadelphia, at Astor House, New York · 1863-12-26

Unread until nowWar & Spies

Historical context

Extraordinary cross-border financial espionage case: paired directly with Marshal Murray's received cipher 7595 (Dec. 30, 1863), revealing that the American Bank Note Co.'s Montreal branch was suspected of engraving the Confederate plates for transit via Rouses Point, Boston, and Cunard steamer to Halifax and Nassau.

Decoded message (plain text)

Washington [Pagan], Dec. 26, 1863, 5 P.M. [Imagine]. For Benjamin Franklin, Esq., Astor House [New York]. This Department has satisfactory evidence that a man named Hilton in New York [Midas] City, supposed to be Winthrop E. Hilton, printer, 11 Spruce St., house 341 East 49th Street according to the address in the Directory, has in his possession machinery for printing Treasury or bank notes for the rebel government, the machinery partly completed and to be shipped from New York [Merlin] about the first of January; also that he has in his possession or within his knowledge a large amount of paper prepared for the same purpose. Prompt and skillful measures should be taken to seize this machinery and paper and to arrest [Wadding] the parties concerned. A letter of Cammack to Memminger, the Secretary of the Treasury [Africa] at Richmond [Neptune], reveals the fact. You will take such measures in conjunction with Marshal Murray as will secure this property and cause the arrest [waggish] of those concerned in the transaction. The letter in my possession says that 'Hilton will have the machinery all finished and the dies all cut by the first of January. The engraving of the plates is superb. They will be shipped by way of Halifax and all according to instructions. The main part of the work has been under the immediate supervision of Hilton, who will act in good faith in consequence of the large amount he has and will receive. The work is beautifully done and the paper is superb. A part has been shipped and the balance will be forwarded in a few days. Send someone to Nassau to receive and take the machinery and paper through Florida.'
