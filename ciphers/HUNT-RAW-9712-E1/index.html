Historical context

Encrypted 1864 War Department dispatch linking Washington to New York, Boston, New England, or Mid-Atlantic port and maritime intelligence hubs monitoring Atlantic raiders, blockade runners, counter-espionage, and northern homefront security.

Decoded message (plain text)

[no 6. F. M ; Geo D. Sheldon Washn Wash Apr. 20th 4 P. M.] [Apr 20] (4 PM) For Lent [Colonel] H Biggs Chief [Quartermaster]. [Captain] Wise has gone to [New York] and has orders when able to leave to join you at [Monroe] & assist you in management of the fleet. [General] Rucker will send an officer in a Steamer down the [Potomac] and stop all vessels first despatched and ordered to assemble in the [Potomac] and to give them orders to proceed at once to [Monroe] and report to you. you should send a vessel in to the bay to stop all bound for the [Potomac] light and change their destination to [Monroe], that is all vessels chartered for the [Expedition], Those which sailed yesterday and today have already orders for [Monroe]. [Major] Van Vleets list is not yet received, below I give [Captain] Wise list of steamers in [Baltimore] and [Philadelphia]. Besides these [3] [Ferry] boats and [3] tugs are on their way to you from [Washington] [Signed] Meigs Render. steamers by [Captain] Wise Steam boats John A. Warner [600] tons [1200] [Men]. Matilda Thunder [100] tons [1500] [Men]. George Weems Tension [100] by [9] tons [800] [Men]. Highland Light [291] tons [600] [Men]. Pioneer [250]. [6] tons [500] [Men]. [Concentrate]er [400]
