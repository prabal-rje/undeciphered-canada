1865-05-04 · Border, Civil War & Confederation (1816-1872)

Mole in New York Post Office ('clerk named Webb') tipped off rebels in Canada ('Canny day') that federal agents are reading their mail

Robert Murray, U.S. Marshal (from Washington, D.C.) to John A. Kennedy, Superintendent of Metropolitan Police, New York · 1865-05-04

Historical context

Sent two days after President Andrew Johnson's May 2, 1865 assassination bounty proclamation naming the Confederate agents in Canada (Thompson, Clay, Tucker, Sanders, Cleary). Caught by our phonetic-split sweep ('Canny day'), exposing an insider counterintelligence leak inside the New York Post Office.

Decoded message (plain text)

Washington, May 4, [1865]. John A. Kennedy. A man by the name of Webb, a clerk in the [stephen] New York [frog] Post Office, has given the rebels [walnuts] in Canada [Canny day] information [Sweden] that we are examining their [transcribed these] letters. Ascertain if a person of that name is employed there. Answer. Signed [Webster], Robert Murray. Please deliver in person and get an immediate answer.
