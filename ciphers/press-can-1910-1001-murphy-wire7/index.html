Ottawa-Vancouver Secret Code Telegram: Laurier ('Smith') Sustains O'Hara ('Hudson') Over Templeman and Murphy on 'Boxes', 'Bales', 'Cedar', and 'Tea'

1910-10-01

Government & Politics

Historical context

The densest single dispatch in the 1910 Ottawa-Vancouver secret codebook, packing eleven codebook terms into fifty words. It documents a high-level federal cabinet clash in which Prime Minister Sir Wilfrid Laurier ('Smith') backed Deputy Minister F. C. T. O'Hara ('Hudson') against two sitting Cabinet ministers, William Templeman ('Temple') and Secretary of State Charles Murphy ('Murphy'), while directing Vancouver agents to pursue the opium investigation ('tea trail') via informant 'Cedar'.

Decoded message (plain text)

Private. Surveyor of Customs Col. Charles A. Worsnop asked Deputy Minister F. C. T. O'Hara to retain regular department counsel. O'Hara refused. Hon. William Templeman and Hon. Charles Murphy (Secretary of State) insisted, but Prime Minister Sir Wilfrid Laurier sustained O'Hara. Worsnop was instructed to prevent the passport migrants' counsel from privately interviewing detained suspects. Tell the undercover informant ('Cedar') to keep an eye on this. Also continue the opium trail. Inspector Foster's authority over Worsnop was confirmed this morning. Signed T. R. E. McInnes.

Original secret code

Ottawa, October 1, 1910. Gordon Grant, Barrister, Williams Building, Vancouver, B.C. Private. Worsnop asked Hudson to retain Road counsel. Hudson refused. Temple and Murphy insisted but Smith sustained Hudson. Worsnop instructed prevent Boxes counsel privately interviewing Bales. Tell Cedar keep eye on this. Also continue tea trail. Hunter's authority over Worsnop confirmed this morning. BROWN.
