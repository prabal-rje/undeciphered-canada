21 October 1901 · Laurier & World War I (1897-1919)

Clifford Sifton's quiet plan to replace B.C.'s Premier without a vote

Interior Minister Clifford Sifton (Ottawa) to PM Wilfrid Laurier (Halifax) · 21 October 1901

Unread until nowGovernment & PoliticsOttawa, ON → Halifax, N.S. (re: Victoria, B.C.)

Historical context

Sifton wired Laurier in Halifax urging him to lean on British Columbia's Lieutenant-Governor, deny Premier James Dunsmuir an election, and hand Victoria to barrister E. V. Bodwell. Right underneath Sifton's signature on the CPR sheet, Laurier pencilled a quiet refusal and crossed it through: "going back straight to Ottawa, will see you then will do nothing now."

Decoded message (plain text)

Fears expressed Dunsmuir may resign and that Lieutenant Governor, not knowing situation, may be persuaded to call on Eberts and grant dissolution; result would be serious confusion. Believe considerable majority support Bodwell, who can carry on Government without new election. Can you communicate with Governor? No commitment to Bodwell policy involved.

Bilge contrition masticated taming Bodwell, writing carrot centennial outlive guarded caleb nunnery encroach. carrot jane conchological asa guardedly?
