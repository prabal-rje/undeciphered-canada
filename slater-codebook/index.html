Undeciphered.ca/The 63-Year Codebook

1873 to 1936 · Canadian Prime Ministerial Cryptography

Five Prime Ministers, one 1870 pocket dictionary

For sixty-three years, from Sir John A. Macdonald through Wilfrid Laurier, Robert Borden, Arthur Meighen, and William Lyon Mackenzie King, Ottawa encrypted confidential cabinet telegrams by shifting words forward or backward inside an 1870 Victorian pocket book: Slater's Telegraphic Code (25,000 numbered English words).

Timeline of the 63-year codebook

  1. 1871 · Pre-Slater

    R. W. Scott and Sir John A. Macdonald

    Before Slater took over Ottawa, Scott asked Macdonald to wire his reply using a Caesar shift (S for A, Pope pp. 153-154).

  2. 1873-1891 · BASE ± DAY

    Sir John A. Macdonald's 122-Wire Corpus

    Matthew Brown (2021 prior art) cracked Macdonald's wires using dynamic daily offsets per recipient (BASE ± DAY: Tupper 1234 + day, Scarth −3456 + day, Van Horne −1000 − day).

  3. July 1877 · +100

    B.C. Indian Reserve Commission

    Anderson and Sproat to David Mills and R. W. Scott (RG 10 Vol. 3651, C-10114), verified exact at Slater +100 decode (secreted → send, pleurisy → police, tease → the).

  4. Mar & Nov 1885 · +45 / +325

    North-West Resistance and Louis Riel Execution Day

    Clarke's Batoche warning (e011166699, +45) and Dewdney's Riel execution wire (e011166694, +325: "Hanged buried as directed... Bonny Face"). Direct scan inspection corrects LAC's 2016 avenger (01854) to avenged (01853), tightening Brown's decode to 12/12 exact.

  5. Dec 1895 · +2002

    Sir Mackenzie Bowell's "McGee Cipher"

    Bowell to Tupper (C-3206 p. 25), named after Privy Council Clerk John J. McGee, identified as Slater +2002 (alongside the January 1895 Newfoundland Bank Crash wire at +7).

  6. 1898-1908 · −400 to +310

    Sir Wilfrid Laurier's Cabinet and Imperial Network

    Eight ciphertext-only Laurier dispatches across Halifax (−400), Washington (−388), Blair and Sifton (−200), London Pacific Cable (−150, seven cables), Yukon (+30), and Frank Oliver (+310), plus Laurier to Sifton (C-372 p. 322, #225530, Slater −200) explicitly encoding "Inform him I use two hundred."

  7. 13-14 May 1911 · −300

    Mackenzie King and Sir Frederick Borden Aboard S.S. Virginian

    King's glossed 13 May shipboard Marconi wireless (C-1914 #15009-15010, 62/67 exact) and Borden's unglossed 14 May reply via Cape Race (#15012), both at Slater −300.

  8. 1914-1919 · +61, −1110, −150 & −401

    Sir Robert Borden's WWI War Cabinet and Paris Peace Delegation

    Four blind-recovered unglossed wires plus thirteen key-verified cables map Borden's wartime four-key network: Day One of WWI to CPR President Shaughnessy (+61, C-4388 #105923), Perley (−1110 in 1914-16; −401 on SS Lord Ormonde in 1918 and Paris Peace talks in 1919), Overseas Minister Kemp (−150, Witley Camp crisis), and party codenames Zornwagon (= Sir George Foster) and Zornwet (= Sir Lomer Gouin).

  9. 1920-1926 · Periods 2, 3, 4 & 10

    Arthur Meighen's Rotating Keys, Rhodes Cycle and Party Supplement

    Meighen's office pushed Slater into rotating keys ([−34, −68], [−21, −53, −96], 241 / 109, and Period-10 Yale) plus a secret Conservative Party proper-noun supplement (Zandrag, Zornwhite). Nova Scotia Premier E. N. Rhodes's replicated Period-3 cycle [−723, +107, −91] appears across both 21 October 1925 (C-3443 #040256, 34 words) and 6 July 1926 (C-3477 #084257), alongside W. H. Dennis from the Ritz-Carlton Montreal (C-3477 #084256, −411), McConnell (−409), Chaplin (−245), Tolmie (−200), and Cantley (20 Sept. 1926, C-3550 p. 65, −20).

  10. Dec 1927 · −401

    Peter C. Larkin (London) to PM Mackenzie King

    London financial wire (MG 26 J 11) verified 21/21 exact on printed Slater plates at −401.

  11. 1920s-1930s · Exact 5-Digit Slater

    St. Pierre and Miquelon Prohibition Rum-Runners

    The Andrieux worksheet published by Smithsonian proves Atlantic rum-runners off Canada used Slater's exact five-digit numbers (09330 For, 10067 God, 19855 sake, 22484 14703 To Morrow).

  12. 7 July 1936 · PMO ±300 Rule

    Office of the Prime Minister Confidential Memo

    LAC MG 26 J 4, Vol. 203, #C140541 confirms 63 continuous years of prime ministerial Slater use: "The Code Number for use in this office is three hundred (300) i.e., add 300 to code, substract 300 to decode." King still noted carrying his Slater Code on his April 1940 trip to meet FDR.
