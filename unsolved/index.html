Still unsolved: Colin Robertson's 1819 Fort Chipewyan captivity code

During the Athabasca fur-trade war of 1818-1819, Hudson's Bay Company officer Colin Robertson was seized and locked in a cabin at Fort Chipewyan (northern Alberta) by rivals from the North West Company. He smuggled coded correspondence out of captivity using a cipher table preserved at the Hudson's Bay Company Archives.

1898 · Montreal Spanish Spy Ring (Bonilla → Victoria, B.C.)

Two Spanish intelligence telegrams sent 5 and 13 July 1898 during the Spanish-American War. Contemporary accounts report U.S. War Department cryptographers never broke the code.

1890-1891 · Matthew Brown's Last 2 Unsolved Macdonald Wires

Macdonald to Bowell (21 July 1890) and Scarth to Campbell (21 March 1891). Every word (6/6 and 5/5) appears in Slater yet resists all 25,000 single shifts, pointing to cursive misreads on film or an early rotating key.

LAC 559036 · LAC 559790

1756 & 1812-1814 · New France Key Shipment & War of 1812 Controls

In New France registers, the 1 April 1756 outgoing dispatch shipping Vaudreuil his cipher key sits at ANOM COL B 103 f. 153r / f. 125r (Envoi d'un chiffre). In War of 1812 files, Baker's 24 August 1812 warning leaves four trailing groups (73 1182 4117 1032); Thomas Barclay's 1813 cross-witness (RG 8 C.689 p. 195 & C.690 p. 109) accounts for six dash positions (2652 stop, 3060 the/they/their, 198 attack); and Albert Gallatin's 13 June 1814 London cipher matches 27/32 cells in U.S. State Dept. table WE028.

ANOM COL B 103 f. 153r · LAC RG 8 C.688 p. 500 · C.689 p. 195 · C.690 p. 109 · WE028

1903 Barton Rosetta, Diplomatic Leads & 1941-1944 WWII Intercepts

Australian PM Edmund Barton's 18 March 1903 wire to Laurier (C-799 #71199-71200) now matches exact printed plaintext in 1903 Sessional Paper No. 89, p. 2, giving a verified Imperial/Colonial Office codebook Rosetta (famegiving = full stop, levelism = N.S.W., recant = South Australia, saintly = terminated by). Open retrieval targets include 1794-95 Simcoe letters (MG 23 H I 1), 1906 Earl Grey Niagara cipher (Durham GRE/B247/8), the 1941 Canadian intercept CDWI00052 (RG 24 C-8335), and the 25 January 1944 VENONA Ottawa GRU 3-group Polish excerpt (74389 13376 ... 48817, 3/NBF/T1521-T1522).
